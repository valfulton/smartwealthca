Is Your Commercial Loan Maturing? Options for Bay Area Property Owners in 2026

By BRG Realty Business & Commercial Team · October 6, 2026 · 6 min read

Thousands of commercial mortgages written when rates were near historic lows are now reaching maturity. In the Bay Area, Trepp tracked roughly $3.18 billion across 87 securitized loans maturing as of March 2026, and that doesn't count bank and private loans. If your loan is coming due, here are your options.

1. Refinance

Lenders size new loans on today's rates and your property's current net operating income (NOI). A loan that was easy to qualify for at 3.5% may need a larger paydown at today's rates. Start 9–12 months before maturity so you have time to shop lenders, including SBA 504 if you occupy the building.

2. Extend or modify

Many lenders would rather extend a performing loan than take a property back. An extension can buy time to lease up vacancy or wait for better rates, often in exchange for a paydown or higher rate.

3. Bring in capital

Preferred equity, mezzanine debt or a new partner can fill the gap between the old loan balance and the new loan amount.

4. Sell

For some owners, especially those with strong equity, selling and doing a 1031 exchange is the cleanest path. Selling on your timeline is almost always better than selling under deadline pressure.

Start with the numbers. We can run a refinance analysis and an opinion of value side by side so you can compare outcomes.
