SBA 7(a) vs. SBA 504: Which Loan Fits Your Business or Building Purchase?

By BRG Realty Business & Commercial Team · October 6, 2026 · 6 min read
ShareLinkedInFacebookXEmailText

The U.S. Small Business Administration's two most popular programs, 7(a) and 504, both help small businesses borrow on better terms than a conventional loan. They are built for different jobs.

At a glance

SBA 7(a)SBA 504
Best forBuying a business, working capital, equipment, partner buyouts, some real estateBuying or building owner-occupied commercial real estate and long-life equipment
MaximumUp to $5 millionSBA/CDC portion up to $5 million ($5.5 million for qualifying manufacturing or energy projects); total project can be larger
Typical structureOne loan from an SBA lenderAbout 50% bank loan, 40% CDC/SBA loan, 10% borrower down payment
RatesUsually variable, tied to primeCDC portion fixed for the life of the loan
GoodwillYes, commonly used for acquisitionsNo

When 7(a) makes sense

If you are buying an existing business, the 7(a) is usually the tool. It can finance goodwill, inventory, equipment and working capital in a single loan, and can include real estate when the business occupies it.

When 504 makes sense

If you are buying the building your business operates in, the 504's low down payment and long fixed rate are hard to beat. Your business generally needs to occupy at least 51% of an existing building, or 60% of new construction.

Program limits, fees and rules change. Always confirm current terms on SBA.gov and with your lender.

Can you use both?

Yes. Buyers sometimes use a 504 for the building and a 7(a) for the business itself. Our commercial loan team can help you compare structures and connect you with SBA lenders and Certified Development Companies serving the Bay Area.
